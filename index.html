How the trick works

  1. Simulate without colour

    Hard disks in a box, half packed into the top-left corner and half into the bottom-right. Every collision, disk on disk or disk on wall, is perfectly elastic and is resolved at the exact moment it happens, so nothing gains or loses energy. Every frame is recorded.

  2. Label at the sort time

    Look at the recorded frame at the sort time. Particles left of the dividing line become blue and the rest become red.

  3. Play the recording back

    The motion is identical, only now it has colours. The gas looks mixed, sorts itself at the sort time, then mixes again.

Why replay a recording?

A gas of colliding disks is chaotic. A difference in the 16th decimal place, from a different time step or frame rate, grows until the two runs have nothing in common after a few dozen collisions. Replaying the recording guarantees you watch the same run that was labelled.

Choosing a sort time

The gas mixes by diffusion, which is slow. If the sort time is too early, the opening frame still remembers the sorted state and the trick shows. The curve above should start and end near 50%. A denser, slower gas or a wider box needs a later sort time.
