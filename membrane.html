How it works

  1. Start mixed

    Particles are scattered at random over the whole box, and exactly half of them, chosen at random, are blue. The rest are red.

  2. Add a one-way membrane

    Blue particles can cross it only leftwards and red ones only rightwards. A particle heading the wrong way bounces off when its edge touches the membrane, exactly as it would off a wall.

  3. Let it run

    Everything else is the same physics as the fake-out: elastic collisions resolved at the exact moment they happen, no damping. The gas sorts itself and stays sorted. Switch the membrane off to watch it mix again.

Why no real membrane can do this

This membrane has to tell each particle's colour and direction and decide whether to let it through. That makes it Maxwell's demon. Real membranes that move one substance in one direction, like the ion pumps in your cells, only manage it by spending energy. A passive one would break the second law of thermodynamics.

Compared with the fake-out

Here colour is a physical property that the membrane acts on, so the sorting is caused. In the fake-out, colour is a label added after the fact, and the motion is an ordinary gas.
